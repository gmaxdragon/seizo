What the simulation can and cannot prove
The PPO policy starts with random weights and selects all five controls from current/previous RGB views and task instructions: base, shoulder, forearm, wrist and gripper. There is no saved target path, inverse-kinematics teacher or scripted rescue. Early discovery rewards are limited to 25 newly visited movement cells per attempt and fade out by 500 episodes. The task reward stays active. Helper views fade only after repeated two-camera validation, not merely after time has passed. The camera policy starts from random weights in its own saved-data area inside this existing installation. The previous hidden-state policy is preserved, not relabeled as vision-trained.
Appearance is photo-based. Two tool servos only: axial tool roll and linked jaw opening. Hardware range, masses, friction and compliant contact forces remain uncalibrated. With MuJoCo installed this is a rigid-body physics simulation with force limits and true box/cylinder/sphere contacts, but uncalibrated joints and approximate arm meshes. The gripper check is a separate scripted mechanism diagnostic, not camera-policy success. Two 7 mm thick rear tabs have estimated placement in camera physics. A simulator-only table-clearance guard can abort an unsafe trial; it is not a physical robot safeguard. The gripper check requires MuJoCo. A mechanism test is not AI learning or physical-arm validation. Objects do not teleport into the gripper, but its contact proxy is simpler than the real jaws. No result enables real motors.
Automatic lessons move from reach, to wrist/aperture, push, lift, place, held-object rotation and oriented placement after held-out validation, while reviewing earlier tasks. Test all tasks evaluates all seven independently from training. You can select a task yourself. One episode ends on success, a fault, or 220 movements. A million episodes is not the same as a million movements.
